# HR Vietnam context

Guidance on Vietnam employment law, statutory social insurance, work permit regulations, and cultural practices for managing workforces in Vietnam. Use alongside core HR skills to adapt corporate policies to Vietnamese labor regulations and local norms.

## Supported tasks

- Applying the Vietnam Labor Code (Law No. 45/2019/QH14) to local employment relationships
- Managing Social Insurance (SI), Health Insurance (HI), and Unemployment Insurance (UI) enrollment and reporting
- Handling personal income tax (PIT) registration, tax code issuing, and payroll tax compliance
- Securing work permits, work permit exemptions, and Temporary Residence Cards (TRC) for foreign employees
- Drafting definite-term and indefinite-term employment contracts under Vietnamese law
- Executing statutory disciplinary procedures, performance termination, and severance pay calculations
- Managing mandatory trade union fee contributions (2%) and worker representative council engagement
- Administering statutory leave entitlements, including annual leave accrual, maternity/paternity leave, and Tet holidays
- Adapting performance reviews and management communication for Vietnamese workplace culture
- Complying with Vietnam data protection regulations (Decree 13/2023/ND-CP) and cybersecurity requirements

## Tips

- **Always formalize employment in writing.** Verbal agreements are legally invalid for contract terms of one month or longer. Every employment relationship must be executed via a written or recognized electronic contract on or before the employee's first working day.
- **Observe the 30-day statutory enrollment window.** Social Insurance (SI), Health Insurance (HI), Unemployment Insurance (UI), and Personal Income Tax (PIT) registrations must be completed within 30 days of contract execution. Late filings result in mandatory back contributions and administrative penalties.
- **Follow statutory disciplinary procedures strictly.** Labor discipline and unilateral terminations require exact compliance with Article 122 of the Labor Code (5 working days advance written notice, mandatory trade union/worker representative participation, signed minutes, and formal written decision). Procedural errors render terminations illegal regardless of cause.
- **Remit the mandatory 2% Trade Union fee.** Registered employers in Vietnam must pay a monthly trade union fee equal to 2% of the total payroll used for social insurance. This fee is obligatory whether or not a local trade union chapter has been formed at the company.
- **Account for foreign worker pre-approval timelines.** Work permit applications require submitting a foreign labor demand explanation (giai trinh nhu cau su dung lao dong nuoc ngoai) and posting local job announcements at least 15 days prior to submission. Plan the full immigration process 60–90 days ahead of the anticipated start date.
- **Structure Tet bonus policies and communication clearly.** While a 13th-month salary is not strictly mandated by statutory law unless included in contracts or company policy, paying a Tet bonus before Lunar New Year is a crucial cultural norm. Define calculation formulas clearly and pair policy rollouts with indirect-feedback-friendly 1-on-1 manager check-ins.
