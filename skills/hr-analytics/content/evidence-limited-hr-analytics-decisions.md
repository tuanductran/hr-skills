# Evidence-limited HR analytics decisions

## Purpose

HR analytics is most useful when it improves a defined decision and makes uncertainty visible. A dashboard or model should not turn incomplete workforce data into a confident story about individual people or groups.

## Decision brief

Before analysis, record the decision, audience, population, time period, definitions, data owner, sources, access boundary, and action that the evidence could support. Distinguish descriptive patterns, explanatory hypotheses, forecasts, and recommendations. Assign the decision to a named HR or business owner rather than leaving the dashboard to speak for itself.

## Evidence quality and limits

Check completeness, consistency, missingness, selection effects, small samples, changing definitions, time lag, and whether a measure is a proxy rather than the outcome of interest. State what the data cannot establish. Combine quantitative results with appropriately governed qualitative context, and avoid presenting correlation as an explanation without further evidence.

## Fair subgroup interpretation

When comparing groups, define the comparison purpose, protect sensitive attributes, and consider role mix, location, tenure, access to opportunity, work arrangement, and sample size. A difference is a question for investigation, not proof of bias or individual risk. Avoid individual-level labels that could trigger employment action without meaningful human review and an approved process.

## Action and monitoring

For each proposed action, record the owner, rationale, expected outcome, affected people, mitigation, evidence threshold, review date, and pause trigger. Monitor whether the action improves the intended outcome without creating avoidable privacy, accessibility, fairness, workload, or trust problems. Retire a metric or model when its purpose, data quality, or decision value no longer holds.
